At a glance

Click any row or tile for its chart and key figures; indexes also show the constituents that moved them, where weights are available. Cash indexes do not trade outside their session. While an exchange is shut, "Since close" shows how far a future or US-listed ETF on the same market has moved since the closing bell, as a guide to where the index would stand now. ETFs are priced in US dollars, so that figure includes currency moves, and it stays empty until the future or ETF has traded after the close.

Quotes: CNBC public feed. US cash indexes, ETFs, currencies and crypto are real-time; other indexes, futures and commodities are delayed by roughly 10–20 minutes. Hover a trend line for its values; times are in your local time. Session status follows regular exchange hours and does not know about half-days.

Short term shows the change over 1 day, 5 days, 1 month and 3 months, with a 3-month trend line; long term shows 6 months, 1 year, 5 years and 10 years, with a 10-year line (or as much history as there is). Five days is measured from the close five sessions before the latest one; longer periods from the last close on or before the same date that long ago (the last weekly close for 5 and 10 years). The 52-week range comes from a year of daily highs and lows. Futures use the continuous front-month contract, so their long-period changes include contract rolls. A dash means the feed's history does not reach back that far. Treasury yields are in percent and their moves in basis points (1 bp = 0.01 of a percentage point); bond indexes are shown through the ETFs that track them.
